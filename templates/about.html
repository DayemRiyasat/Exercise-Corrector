How this actually works

No black box. Here is the pipeline, the models behind it, and an honest account of where it falls short.

The pipeline

From a camera frame to a correction on screen

  1. 1Your browser captures a frame from the webcam, video file or photo you gave it.
  2. 2The frame goes to the server, where a YOLO11 pose model finds {{ site_stats.landmarks }} body joints.
  3. 3The joints are cleaned up over time: gaps are filled and jitter is smoothed out.
  4. 4A rep counter follows how high the body is and marks where each rep starts, bottoms out and finishes. Jump rope and plank have no reps, so they are cut into 2 second windows instead.
  5. 5Every finished rep or window is judged as a whole: rules measure depth, knee and hip angles, and a neural network trained on thousands of labelled reps looks at the full movement. Their votes are combined into a verdict, named faults and a score out of 100.
  6. 6The verdict is matched to coaching copy and shown on screen as soon as the rep or window ends.
What it does well
  • Catching the common, visible faults a coach would call out across the gym floor
  • Counting reps without you touching anything mid-set
  • Giving feedback fast enough to change the next rep
  • Keeping an honest record of whether a fix is holding over weeks
Where it falls short
  • It only sees what the camera sees. Bad framing produces bad calls, not no calls.
  • It has no idea about pain, injury history or your individual anatomy.
  • It judges position, not intent. A deliberately different variation may read as a fault.
  • It cannot see load. A clean rep at a dangerous weight still reads as clean.
