How this actually works

No black box. Here is the pipeline, the models behind it, and an honest account of where it falls short.

The pipeline

From a camera frame to a correction on screen

  1. 1Your browser captures a frame from the webcam, video file or photo you gave it.
  2. 2MediaPipe Pose, running locally in the page, finds {{ site_stats.landmarks }} body landmarks and returns their x, y and z positions.
  3. 3Those coordinates alone are posted to the server. The image is discarded and never leaves your machine.
  4. 4A neural network trained for your chosen exercise turns the coordinates into features and classifies the position, returning a label and a confidence score.
  5. 5A per-exercise rep counter tracks the movement phase and increments only on a full range of motion.
  6. 6The label is matched to coaching copy and rendered on screen, roughly {{ site_stats.checks_per_second }} times a second.
What it does well
  • Catching the common, visible faults a coach would call out across the gym floor
  • Counting reps without you touching anything mid-set
  • Giving feedback fast enough to change the next rep
  • Keeping an honest record of whether a fix is holding over weeks
Where it falls short
  • It only sees what the camera sees. Bad framing produces bad calls, not no calls.
  • It has no idea about pain, injury history or your individual anatomy.
  • It judges position, not intent. A deliberately different variation may read as a fault.
  • It cannot see load. A clean rep at a dangerous weight still reads as clean.
